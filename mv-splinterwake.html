Splinterwake: A Guide to the Broken Multiverse

Welcome to the Bleed.

Forty years ago, the sky did not fall. It split.

In the history books, it is called the Sundering. It was not a meteor, a war, or a plague. It was the catastrophic shattering of the Membrane, the metaphysical seal that kept our universe distinct from the billions of others that existed alongside it. When the Membrane broke, reality didn’t just break; it leaked. Skyscrapers from one Earth intersected with pre-industrial villages from another. Oceans merged, birthing hybrid ecosystems and tidal monsters. But the most terrifying consequence was not physical—it was personal. Human consciousness began to bleed. People started remembering lives they never lived, seeing the countless other versions of themselves walking beside them.

Society didn’t collapse into war; it collapsed into ontological confusion. The old nations dissolved, replaced by Enclaves carved out of the chaos. From the ruins, three powerful factions emerged, each believing they had found the key to controlling the bleeding. This is the world of Splinterwake: a place where geography is fluid, identity is fragile, and time is a weapon.

The Three Factions

The stability of the multiverse hangs in the balance, contested by three groups who have mastered different aspects of the rupture.

The Cartographers believe in order. Their goal is the Stable Atlas, a fixed multiverse where locations are permanent and predictable. Using Spatial Anchoring, they pin specific places from one Earth to another, folding space to create safe zones and moving entire cities to prevent collapse. Led by the precise technocrat Director Elias Thorne, they view people as variables to be managed. However, their internal conflict is sharp: the "Pruners" want to erase unstable zones to preserve the Atlas, a policy many moderates call genocide.

The Weavers believe in unity. They argue that the pain of the Sundering stems from fragmented selfhood. Their practice, Soul-Splicing, allows them to transfer memories, traits, and loyalties between duplicates, creating "Chord Groups" that operate in perfect unison. They are led not by a single person, but by The Consensus, a rotating council of seven high-level duplicates who share a brain-trust. Their current voice is Elara Vane. But there is a dark undercurrent: a growing faction of "Originals" who refuse to be spliced, viewing the Weavers as slave-masters of the mind.

The Chronoclade believes in preservation. They seek to seal time, either by freezing the multiverse in a pre-Sundering "Golden Moment" or by pruning all but one timeline to prevent future errors. Using Temporal Looping, they can trap locations in repeating cycles or age objects by moving them between temporal strata. Their leader, Abbess Penultima, is an ancient figure who views time as a garden requiring ruthless weeding. Within the Clade, the "Loopers" (who want to freeze the present) and the "Pruners" (who want to delete history) are locked in a deadly, philosophical schism.

The Protagonists

At the center of this fractured world are three individuals whose fates are inextricably linked.

Tamsin Vreel is a defector from the Cartographers. A former Anchor, she was responsible for an operation that killed thousands due to a miscalculation. Driven by guilt and a desire for a "wild" multiverse where no single faction controls geography, she stole a rare Anchor-Crystal and fled. She possesses limited spatial folding abilities, but using them drains her physical form, causing her skin to become translucent and her body to risk Spatial Unmooring.

Kellan Irie is an Unmoored smuggler. Left in a "null zone" for three days during the initial Sundering, Kellan lost his connection to any specific Earth. He is ontologically scrambled, belonging to no timeline, which makes him invisible to faction detection systems. He navigates the Splinterwake by instinct, reading the bleed to find safe paths. He is motivated by survival and curiosity, though his fragmented identity makes him vulnerable to the Weavers’ splicing.

Abbess Penultima is the face of the Chronoclade. Over 300 years old, her body is held together by temporal stasis fields, though her hands are constantly crumbling into dust—a condition known as Rust. She is not a traditional villain; she genuinely believes that free will caused the Sundering and that only strict temporal order can save existence. Her goal is to initiate "The Great Pause," freezing the world in a state of pre-disaster peace.

How to Read This Serial

Splinterwake is an eight-chapter serial designed to be read in sequence, as each chapter tightens the noose around the three factions. The story moves through key locations like the Spire of Ombra, a neutral trading ground that exists on three Earths simultaneously, and the Glass Desert, a graveyard of failed temporal experiments where time is crystallized.

The narrative is a race against the collapse of reality. As the factions clash, the costs of their manipulations become visceral: Displacement Sickness for the Cartographers, Echo Decay for the Weavers, and Temporal Paradox Rot for the Chronoclade.

This is a story for those who love hard sci-fi, high-stakes conspiracy, and the existential horror of losing oneself. Whether you are new to multiverse lore or a seasoned traveler of parallel worlds, Splinterwake invites you to step through the bleed. The map is changing, the timeline is fraying, and the only way to find your way out is to read on.
