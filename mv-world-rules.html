The Seamburst

Overview

The Seamburst, historically dated to forty years prior to the present era, marks the catastrophic failure of the Membrane—the metaphysical substrate that previously sealed the multiverse as an archive of parallel timelines. Unlike a natural disaster, the Seamburst was a structural collapse of reality’s continuity. The Membrane did not merely break; it shattered into billions of shards, creating Fracture Zones where the boundaries between adjacent Earths dissolved entirely.

The immediate aftermath was defined by ontological chaos rather than simple physical destruction. Infrastructure from distinct epochs and dimensions intersected; skyscrapers fused with pre-industrial villages, and oceans merged to spawn hybrid ecosystems and tidal monsters. Most significantly, human consciousness began to "bleed." Individuals experienced memories of lives they had not lived and discovered that their identity was merely one iteration among thousands, now visible and interactable. This ontological confusion caused the collapse of pre-Seamburst nations, which were subsequently replaced by Enclaves—territories established by those capable of controlling the local bleed.

The Bleeding Multiverse

Following the initial collapse, the multiverse entered a state of unstable flux. The Fracture Zones are not static; they are dynamic areas where the laws of physics, history, and spatial coordinates shift unpredictably. This phenomenon is referred to as the Bleed. The Bleed allows for the exchange of matter, energy, and consciousness across temporal and spatial barriers.

The Bleed is the primary resource and hazard of the post-Seamburst world. It enables the three dominant factions—the Cartographers, the Weavers, and the Chronoclade—to exert power by manipulating specific types of rupture. However, uncontrolled Bleed leads to "ontological noise," where reality loses its coherence, resulting in the spontaneous emergence of paradoxes, duplicated matter, and the erosion of individual identity.

Rules and Costs of Manipulation

The power to manipulate the broken reality is categorized into three distinct disciplines, each governed by specific laws and exacting physical or psychological costs.

### Cartography (Spatial Manipulation)

The Cartographers practice Spatial Anchoring, the process of pinning a location from one Earth to another to create stability. They fold space to create shortcuts, move entire cities, and "flatten" unstable zones.

  • Rule: Distance is relative to mass and intent. Moving large masses requires exponentially more energy than moving individuals.
  • Cost: Practitioners suffer from Displacement Sickness, characterized by severe vertigo and nausea. Prolonged use leads to Spatial Unmooring. In this state, the user’s physical form loses cohesion, becoming transparent and intangible. If unchecked, the user phases entirely out of existence, ceasing to occupy any spatial coordinate.

    • ### Weaving (Psychological/Self Manipulation)

    The Weavers engage in Soul-Splicing, a technique that transfers memories, loyalties, and physical traits between duplicates. They seek "Psychic Harmony" by merging fragmented identities into a cohesive whole, often creating "Chord Groups" of synchronized duplicates.

  • Rule: Identity is a narrative construct. Changing a core memory fundamentally alters the subject’s future behavior and self-concept.
  • Cost: Splicing accelerates Echo Decay. The more identities are merged, the more the operator’s original self erodes. Users begin to forget their own names, faces, and histories, becoming hollow vessels for the dominant narrative. The extreme end-stage is The Hollow, a state of total amnesia and catatonic emptiness where the individual is a blank slate, devoid of agency.

    • ### Chronoclade (Temporal Manipulation)

    The Chronoclade utilizes Temporal Looping and Era Sealing to bend time. They can trap locations in loops, age or youthify matter, and attempt to freeze history into a singular "Golden Moment."

  • Rule: Time flows forward but can be bent, not broken. Causality remains a rigid structure that resists reversal.
  • Cost: Temporal manipulation generates Temporal Paradox Rot. Micro-contradictions cause disproportionate cellular aging; some cells may age centuries while others remain in an infantile state. This results in Rust, a rapid, grotesque disintegration where the body crumbles as time devours it from the inside out.

    • The Unmoored

    A rare and dangerous class of individuals known as The Unmoored exist on the fringes of the multiverse. These are persons who, during the initial Seamburst, were trapped in "ashmere verges" where the laws of physics suspended. They lost their connection to any specific Earth or timeline.

    The Unmoored possess a scrambled ontological signature, rendering them invisible to standard faction detection systems. This makes them invaluable as smugglers and spies, as they can navigate Splinterwake intuitively without triggering spatial alarms. However, their fragmented identity makes them prime targets for the Weavers, who view them as malleable raw material for splicing. Notable Unmoored, such as Kellan Irie, survive through stealth and the constant management of their own dissipating reality.

    Key Locations

    The post-Seamburst world is defined by several critical sites where the Bleed is most intense or controlled.

  • The Spire of Ombra: A massive vertical city existing simultaneously on three different Earths. It serves as the neutral ground for inter-faction trade. Its architecture is unstable, shifting appearance and structure depending on the perspective of the observer’s home Earth.
  • The Cinderglass Waste: A Fracture Zone where time has crystallized into physical sand. Nothing within the Desert can be destroyed, only preserved. It serves as a graveyard for failed temporal experiments and a high-risk arena for Chronoclade skirmishes.
  • The Weaver’s Loom: A hidden sub-basement network beneath the Spire. Here, thousands of duplicates are kept in stasis, their minds merged to power the Weavers’ ideologic network. It is the source of the "Consensus" and a site of profound ethical horror.
  • The Ashmere Verge: An area of pure chaos where no laws of physics apply. It functions as a blind spot in the multiverse, utilized by the Unmoored as a refuge and by factions as a location for activities that cannot be tracked or reversed.
