Chronoclade

Not to be confused with: Temporal Displacement Sickness, The Great Pause

The Chronoclade is one of the three dominant geopolitical and metaphysical factions that emerged in the immediate aftermath of the Seamburst. While The Cartographers focus on spatial stability and The Weavers on psychic cohesion, the Chronoclade is defined by its absolute dominion over time. Viewed with a mixture of horror and reverence by the other powers, the Chronoclade operates under the doctrine that history is not a river to be navigated, but a garden that requires ruthless, constant weeding.

Origins

The origins of the Chronoclade are shrouded in the ontological confusion of the post-Seamburst era. As the Membrane shattered, creating billions of Fracture Zones, the faction did not emerge from a single political center but coalesced around those who discovered they could manipulate the temporal substrate of reality.

Historical records suggest that the first known Chronoclade practitioners were scholars and monks who had retreated to isolated monasteries prior to the Seamburst. As the boundaries between adjacent Earths dissolved, these individuals realized that the chaotic leakage of time allowed for the preservation of moments. They formed their order not as a state, but as a religious-technological hierarchy, establishing their headquarters in the Cinderglass Waste, a Fracture Zone where sand is made of crystallized time and nothing can be destroyed, only preserved. This environment became their laboratory and their graveyard, filled with the remains of failed temporal experiments.

Goals

The primary objective of the Chronoclade is the cessation of temporal entropy. The faction seeks to achieve what is internally termed the Golden Moment—a state where the multiverse is frozen in a perfect, static configuration.

There are two prevailing interpretations of this goal within the organization:

1. Restoration: Reverting the timeline to the moment immediately preceding the Seamburst, effectively undoing the disaster and preserving the pre-fracture peace.

2. Optimization: Pruning all but the "perfect" timeline to prevent future errors, ensuring that only a single, curated history remains.

Both goals stem from the belief that free will and the unpredictable flow of time led to the Seamburst. The Chronoclade views the chaotic existence of multiple, interacting timelines as a disease that threatens the coherence of reality. Their ultimate endgame, known as The Great Pause, is a global time-loop that would freeze the world in a state of eternal, unchanging peace, eliminating conflict, decay, and death, albeit at the cost of progression and change.

Methods

The Chronoclade possesses unique capabilities in temporal manipulation, governed by the rule that time can be bent, but not broken. Their primary methods include:

### Era Sealing

This technique involves isolating a specific region or event from the flow of linear time. By sealing an era, the Chronoclade can preserve a location in its current state indefinitely, preventing decay or external interference. This is often used to protect sensitive archives or to create static buffer zones around unstable Fractures.

### Temporal Looping

Perhaps their most feared capability, Temporal Looping traps a location or group of individuals in a repeated cycle of time. The loop continues until a specific condition is met or the target is "resolved." This method is used for interrogation, training, and as a weapon. Survivors of short-duration loops often suffer from severe psychological trauma, as they are forced to experience the same traumatic events repeatedly.

### Timeline Pruning

A more aggressive and controversial method, Pruning involves the selective deletion of timelines or historical centuries. By moving subjects or objects between temporal strata, Chronoclade agents can "age" or "youthify" entities, or entirely erase specific eras from the record. This process is highly unstable and carries significant risks of paradox.

The cost of such manipulation is severe. Chronoclade practitioners suffer from Temporal Paradox Rot, a condition where the body ages disproportionately. Cells age centuries while others remain youthful, leading to Rust—a grotesque physical disintegration where the body crumbles as time devours it from the inside out.

Leader and Key Members

The faction is led by Abbess Penultima, an ancient figure who has existed across multiple centuries. Held together by complex temporal stasis fields, Penultima is over 300 years old. Her hands are constantly crumbling into dust due to advanced Rust, requiring her to constantly repair her own timeline to maintain coherence. She views her role not as leadership, but as stewardship, believing that only strict temporal order can save existence from the chaos of free will.

Other key members include the Loopers, a subset of agents who specialize in trapping targets in time loops, and the Pruners, radical operatives who advocate for the aggressive deletion of entire centuries to streamline the timeline.

Internal Tensions

The Chronoclade is plagued by a profound philosophical schism that has led to frequent, deadly skirmishes within its ranks.

  • The Loopers: This faction advocates for freezing the current moment. They believe that any change, no matter how small, risks further instability. Their goal is to maintain the status quo indefinitely, prioritizing safety over progress.
  • The Pruners: Conversely, the Pruners argue that the current timeline is too corrupted. They seek to delete entire centuries of history to "cleanse" the record and create a linear, error-free narrative. They view the Loopers as cowardly preservers of a broken world.

    • This tension creates an environment of extreme paranoia. Agents must constantly verify the "integrity" of their colleagues, as both factions believe the other is a threat to the stability of the multiverse.

    Relationships with Other Factions

    The Chronoclade maintains a tense, adversarial relationship with the other two major factions.

  • The Cartographers: The Cartographers, led by Director Elias Thorne, seek to anchor space. They view the Chronoclade’s temporal manipulation as a threat to the Stable Atlas, as freezing time complicates spatial anchoring. Thorne’s hardliners often accuse the Chronoclade of hoarding time rather than sharing it, leading to frequent border disputes in Fracture Zones where space and time bleed into one another.
  • The Weavers: The Weavers, who seek Psychic Harmony through Soul-Splicing, are in direct conflict with the Chronoclade over the nature of identity. The Weavers believe identity is a narrative that can be rewritten, while the Chronoclade views identity as fixed within the flow of time. The Weavers’ ability to splice memories makes them immune to certain time-loop interrogation techniques, leading to complex espionage games. Furthermore, the Weavers’ leader, The Consensus, views the Chronoclade’s desire to freeze time as a form of mass murder, as it denies the duplicates their ability to evolve or change.

    • Despite these hostilities, all three factions operate from the Spire of Ombra, a vertical city existing simultaneously on three Earths, where neutral ground is maintained through fragile treaties. The Spire serves as a marketplace for rare artifacts, including the Anchor-Crystals and other tools of manipulation, where the factions trade under the watchful eyes of all three leaders.
